# Pinochle

Four-handed partnership auction pinochle. Bid for the right to name trump, score for combinations of cards (meld), then win tricks with points in them.

## Players and cards

- 4 players in two partnerships. Partners sit opposite each other.
- A 48-card pinochle deck: two copies of the 9, J, Q, K, 10 and A of each suit.
- Cards rank, high to low: **A, 10, K, Q, J, 9**. Note the 10 ranks above the king.
- Each player gets 12 cards.

## Bidding

Starting left of the dealer, players bid for the right to name trump. The first bid is at least 20, and each bid must be higher than the last.

- You may pass instead, but once you pass you're out of the auction for this hand.
- If the first three players pass, the dealer must bid.
- The highest bidder wins the auction and names trump. Their side must make the bid.

## Passing cards

The bidder's partner passes 4 cards to the bidder. The bidder looks at them, then passes 4 cards back.

## Meld

Every player then shows their meld, pressing **Show my meld**. The table counts it and lists each one in the log.

- **Run** (A, 10, K, Q, J of trump): 15. A double run: 150.
- **Royal marriage** (K and Q of trump): 4. A run's king and queen don't count again as a royal marriage.
- **Marriage** (K and Q of another suit): 2.
- **Dix** (9 of trump): 1 each.
- **Aces around** (an ace of each suit): 10. Double: 100.
- **Kings around:** 8. Double: 80.
- **Queens around:** 6. Double: 60.
- **Jacks around:** 4. Double: 40.
- **Pinochle** (Q♠ and J♦): 4. Double pinochle: 30.

## Play

The bidder leads the first trick. The winner of each trick leads the next.

- You must follow the suit led if you can.
- If you can't, you must play a trump if you have one.
- You must beat the cards in the trick if you can, including playing a higher trump than one already played.
- The highest trump wins. If no trump was played, the highest card of the suit led wins. When two identical cards are played, the first one played wins.

## Scoring

Each ace, ten and king taken in tricks is worth 1 point, and taking the last trick is worth 1 more. That's 25 points in every hand.

A side's meld only counts if that side takes at least one trick.

- **The bidding side:** if its meld plus trick points reaches the bid, it scores them all. If not, it loses the amount of the bid.
- **The other side:** scores its meld plus trick points.

## Winning

The first side to reach 150 wins. If both sides pass 150 in the same hand, the bidding side wins.

## House rules

- **Passing** (on by default): the 4-card passes between the bidder and their partner. Turn it off to play without passing.
- **Classic scoring** (off by default): aces 11, tens 10, kings 4, queens 3, jacks 2, and the last trick 10. Meld, bids and the target are all ×10 (a run is 150, the lowest bid 200, and the game is to 1,500). Bids go up in tens.
- **Play to:** 150 (the default), 100 or 200 (×10 with classic scoring).
- **Lowest bid:** 20 (the default), 25 or 50 (×10 with classic scoring).
- **The dealer must bid when everyone else passes** (on by default): turn it off and, when everyone passes, the hand is dealt again.
- **The bidder may throw in the hand before play** (off by default): after seeing the meld, the bidder may give up the hand and lose only the bid, without playing it out.
- **You must beat the cards in the trick when you can** (on by default): turn it off and you only need to follow suit, or trump when you can't.
