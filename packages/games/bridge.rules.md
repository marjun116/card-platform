# Bridge

Contract bridge with rubber scoring. Partners bid in an auction for the contract, and then the declarer plays both their own hand and their partner's, the dummy, which everyone can see.

## Players and cards

- 4 players in two partnerships. Partners sit opposite each other.
- One standard deck. Aces are high. Each player gets 13 cards.
- The deal moves one seat to the left each hand.

## The auction

The dealer calls first, and calls go to the left. On your turn you make one call:

- **Pass.** Passing doesn't take you out; you can bid later.
- **A bid:** a number from 1 to 7 and a suit or no trump (NT). The number is how many tricks over six your side will take. 4♥ means taking 10 tricks with hearts as trump. Each bid must be higher than the last: a higher number, or the same number in a higher suit. Suits rank ♣, ♦, ♥, ♠, then NT.
- **Double** the other side's last bid, making it worth more if it's made, and costlier if it isn't.
- **Redouble** your side's bid after the other side has doubled it.

The auction ends when three players pass in a row after a bid. The last bid is the **contract**. If all four players pass at the start, the hand is thrown in.

**The declarer** is the player on the winning side who first named the contract's suit (or NT). Their partner is the **dummy**.

## Play

- The player to the declarer's left leads the first trick.
- Then the dummy's cards are laid face up for everyone to see. The declarer plays them as well as their own, and the dummy's player sits out the hand.
- Follow the suit led if you can. If you can't, play any card.
- The highest trump wins. If no trump was played (or the contract is NT), the highest card of the suit led wins. The winner leads next.

## Scoring

Scores are kept in two parts: points **below the line** count toward winning games; points **above the line** are bonuses.

**Making the contract,** below the line, for each trick bid over six:

- ♣ or ♦: 20 each.
- ♥ or ♠: 30 each.
- NT: 40 for the first, 30 for each after that.
- Doubled: ×2. Redoubled: ×4.

**Games and vulnerability:** 100 or more points below the line is a **game**. Both sides then start the next game from zero below the line. A side that has won a game is **vulnerable**, and its bonuses and penalties grow.

**Above the line:**

- **Overtricks** (extra tricks): their trick value, or, when doubled, 100 each (200 vulnerable), twice that redoubled.
- **Making a doubled contract:** 50 (redoubled 100).
- **Small slam** (bid and make 12 tricks): 500, or 750 vulnerable.
- **Grand slam** (bid and make all 13): 1,000, or 1,500 vulnerable.
- **Honors**, scored by whichever side holds them: 4 of the 5 top trumps (A, K, Q, J, 10) in one hand 100, all 5 150, all 4 aces in one hand at NT 150.

**Going down,** to the defenders, for each trick short:

- Not doubled: 50 each (100 vulnerable).
- Doubled, not vulnerable: 100 for the first, 300 for two, 500 for three, and 300 more for each after that.
- Doubled, vulnerable: 200 for the first, 500 for two, 800 for three, and 300 more for each after that.
- Redoubled: twice the doubled amount.

**The rubber:** the first side to win two games wins the rubber and scores 700, or 500 if the other side has a game.

## Winning

When the last rubber ends (one, unless the table chose more), the side with the higher total (above and below the line together) wins.

## House rules

- **Scoring:** "Rubber bridge" (the default), or "Chicago (sets of 4 deals)". In Chicago, vulnerability goes by deal: neither side, the dealer's side, the dealer's side, both sides. Partscores carry over within the set. Making a game scores 300 (500 vulnerable) and clears both sides' partscores. A partscore on the 4th deal that doesn't make a game scores 100. A hand nobody bid on is dealt again by the same dealer and doesn't count as one of the 4.
- **Rubbers to play:** 1 (the default), 2 or 3. With Chicago, this is the number of sets of 4 deals.
- **Honors** (on by default): turn it off to play without honors.
