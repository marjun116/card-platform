# Spades

A partnership trick-taking game. Bid how many tricks your side will take, then take at least that many. Spades are always trump.

## Players and cards

- 4 players in two partnerships. Partners sit opposite each other.
- One standard 52-card deck. Aces are high: A, K, Q, J, 10 down to 2.
- The deal moves one seat to the left each hand. Everyone gets 13 cards.

## Object

Be the first side to reach the target score (500 unless the table chose otherwise).

## Bidding

Starting with the player to the dealer's left, each player bids once: the number of tricks, from 1 to 13, they expect to take. Your bid and your partner's bid add up to your side's bid.

- **Nil** is a bid to take no tricks at all. It counts as 0 toward your side's bid.
- **Blind nil** (only with that house rule) is a nil bid made before you look at your cards. Your cards stay face down until you either bid blind nil or choose to look.

## Play

The player to the dealer's left leads the first trick. After that, whoever won the last trick leads.

- You must follow the suit that was led if you can. If you can't, play any card, including a spade.
- You can't lead a spade until a spade has been played on an earlier trick ("spades are broken"), unless your hand holds nothing but spades.
- The highest spade wins the trick. If no spade was played, the highest card of the suit led wins.

## Scoring

At the end of each hand, each side scores together:

- **Made the bid:** 10 points for each trick bid, plus 1 point for each extra trick. Extra tricks are called **bags**.
- **Fell short:** lose 10 points for each trick bid.
- **Bags add up.** Every time a side collects 10 bags, it loses 100 points and the count starts again.
- **Nil:** 100 points to the side if the nil bidder takes no tricks; 100 off if they take any. The nil bidder's tricks still count toward their partner's bid.
- **Blind nil:** the same, for 200 points.

## Winning

When a side reaches the target score at the end of a hand, the side with the higher score wins. If both sides are tied, another hand is played.

With the mercy rule (on unless the table turned it off), the game also ends when a side falls to −200 or below, and the side with the higher score wins.

## House rules

- **Allow nil bids** (on by default): players may bid nil.
- **Allow blind nil** (off by default): players may bid blind nil, worth 200, before looking at their cards.
- **Play to:** the target score: 300, 500 (the default) or 1,000.
- **End the game when a team falls to -200** (on by default): the mercy rule above. Turn it off to play on however low a side goes.
